# 1. Atoms

<!-- slide -->
## Dalton's atomic theory (1808)
1. Elements are made of atoms
2. All atoms of one element are identical; different elements differ
3. Compounds combine atoms in fixed whole-number ratios
4. Reactions rearrange atoms — never create or destroy them

<!-- slide -->
## Subatomic particles
| Particle | Mass (g) | Charge |
|---|---|---|
| Electron | $9.109\times10^{-28}$ | $-1$ |
| Proton | $1.673\times10^{-24}$ | $+1$ |
| Neutron | $1.675\times10^{-24}$ | $0$ |

## A schematic atom
![Atom structure](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-6fy5f5l8wgbv =380x)
Nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons — over 99.94% of mass is in the nucleus

<!-- slide -->
## Three kinds of radioactive emission
![Alpha, beta and gamma compared](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-e1824q16slq8 =720x)
$\alpha$ = helium nucleus (+2) · $\beta$ = fast electron (−1) · $\gamma$ = high-energy photon (0)

<!-- slide step -->
## Rutherford's gold-foil experiment
![Thomson prediction vs. Rutherford result](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-h1k7wouaepuk =600x)
- **Thomson model predicted:** every particle sails through{{attrs[.fragment]}}
- **Actually observed:** a few deflected sharply, a rare few bounced back{{attrs[.fragment]}}
- Only a small, dense, positively charged nucleus explains it{{attrs[.fragment]}}

<!-- slide -->
## Atomic number, mass number, isotopes
$$Z=\text{protons} \qquad A=\text{protons}+\text{neutrons}$$
**Isotopes:** same Z, different A (different neutron count)

<!-- slide -->
## Reading the notation
![Isotope notation and the isotopes of carbon](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-75ino17y5bdh =700x)
Change $Z$ and it is a different element · change only the neutrons and it is the same element, a different isotope

<!-- slide step -->
## Worked example — U-235
Mass number 235, atomic number 92
- neutrons $=A-Z=235-92$ {{attrs[.fragment]}}
- **= 143**{{attrs[.fragment]}}

<!-- slide -->
## The periodic table
![Periodic table with categories and named families](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-rgp5d1marjmw =860x)
Rows are **periods** · columns are **groups** — and elements in one group behave alike

<!-- slide -->
## Periodic table families
- **Alkali metals** (1A): Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr
- **Alkaline earth metals** (2A): Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra
- **Halogens** (7A): F, Cl, Br, I, At
- **Noble gases** (8A): He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn

# 2. Molecules and Ions

<!-- slide -->
## Molecules
- Diatomic: $\ce{H2}$ · Polyatomic: 3+ atoms
- Only the 6 noble gases exist as lone atoms
- Organic functional groups: hydroxyl (-OH), amino (-NH₂), carboxyl (-COOH)

<!-- slide -->
## Cations and anions
- **Cation** — lost electron(s), net positive: $\ce{Na+}$
- **Anion** — gained electron(s), net negative: $\ce{Cl-}$
![Table salt crystals](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-0gzt5y71lgay =380x)

<!-- slide -->
## The group number gives you the charge
![Ion charges by group](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-bpsbb2ulelh6 =800x)
Metals on the left lose electrons · nonmetals on the right gain them · transition metals vary, hence Roman numerals

<!-- slide step -->
## Worked example — Cl⁻
Chlorine (Z = 17) gains 1 electron
- Protons: unchanged by electron transfer{{attrs[.fragment]}}
- **17 protons, 18 electrons**{{attrs[.fragment]}}

<!-- slide -->
## Molecular vs. empirical formula
- Molecular: exact atom count ($\ce{H2O2}$)
- Empirical: simplest ratio ($\ce{HO}$)
- Glucose $\ce{C6H12O6}$ → empirical $\ce{CH2O}$ (×6)

<!-- slide -->
## Building an ionic formula: charges must cancel
![Charge balance, with and without reduction](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-pa7t3yjb1qt2 =800x)
Cross the charges into subscripts, reduce if they share a factor, then check the sum is zero

<!-- slide step -->
## Worked example — ionic formula
$\ce{Pb^4+}$ and $\ce{O^2-}$
- Cross charges: $\ce{Pb2O4}$ {{attrs[.fragment]}}
- Reduce: **$\ce{PbO2}$** (lead(IV) oxide){{attrs[.fragment]}}

# 3. Naming Compounds

<!-- slide -->
## First decide which system
![Naming decision tree](https://alembic.orz.how/d/doc-67a925ta4uk4 =840x)
Classify first, name second — Roman numerals and Greek prefixes are never mixed

<!-- slide -->
## Ionic compounds
- Metal cation name + nonmetal "-ide"
- Transition metals: Roman numeral for charge
- $\ce{FeCl2}$ = iron(II) chloride; $\ce{FeCl3}$ = iron(III) chloride

<!-- slide step -->
## Worked example — naming
- $\ce{NH4NO3}$ = ammonium + nitrate{{attrs[.fragment]}}
- **ammonium nitrate**{{attrs[.fragment]}}
- $\ce{Fe3(PO4)2}$: $3x+2(-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=+2$ {{attrs[.fragment]}}
- **iron(II) phosphate**{{attrs[.fragment]}}

<!-- slide -->
## Molecular compounds: Greek prefixes
mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, penta-, hexa-, hepta-, octa-, nona-, deca-
$\ce{CO}$ = carbon monoxide; $\ce{CO2}$ = carbon dioxide

<!-- slide -->
## Oxoacids: the reference pattern
5 references: carbonic, chloric, nitric, phosphoric, sulfuric
- **+1 O** → per...-ic
- **−1 O** → -ous
- **−2 O** → hypo...-ous

<!-- slide step -->
## Worked example — hypobromous acid
$\ce{HBrO}$ has 2 fewer O than bromic acid ($\ce{HBrO3}$)
- **hypobromous acid**{{attrs[.fragment]}}

<!-- slide -->
## Bases and hydrates
- Base: yields $\ce{OH-}$ in water ($\ce{NaOH}$)
- Hydrate: fixed attached waters ($\ce{CuSO4\cdot5H2O}$ = copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate)

# From particles to names
Atomic structure explains the periodic table; the periodic table explains how atoms combine into molecules and ions; naming is the mechanical skill that makes every later chapter's formulas and equations readable.
