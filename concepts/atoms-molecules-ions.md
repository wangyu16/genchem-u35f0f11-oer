# Chapter 2 Concept Map: Atoms, Molecules, and Ions

**Reference:** instructor's own lecture notes (Yu Wang, University of Louisiana at Lafayette),

## Chapter Summary

Chapter 2 moves from the macroscopic view of matter (Chapter 1) to the particulate one: matter is
made of atoms, and atoms have internal structure. The chapter builds this structure historically —
Dalton's atomic theory, then the classic experiments (Thomson, Millikan, Rutherford, Chadwick) that
revealed the electron, the nucleus, and the neutron — before formalizing atomic number, mass
number, and isotopes, and introducing the periodic table as an organizing chart.

The second half shifts from single atoms to combinations of atoms: molecules (neutral, covalently
bonded aggregates) and ions (charged species formed by electron loss or gain), including the
distinction between molecular and empirical formulas. The chapter closes with the mechanical skill
of naming compounds — ionic compounds (including transition-metal compounds with Roman numerals),
molecular compounds (Greek-prefix naming), acids (including the oxoacid/oxoanion naming pattern),
bases, and hydrates. This chapter is entirely vocabulary- and structure-building: it supplies the
formulas, names, and particle vocabulary that Chapter 3 (stoichiometry) and Chapter 4 (aqueous
reactions) immediately put to quantitative use.

### 1. Atoms (§2.1)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 2.1a** — State Dalton's atomic theory and describe the subatomic particles
- **Objective 2.1b** — Summarize Rutherford's gold-foil experiment and what it revealed about atomic structure
- **Objective 2.1c** — Define atomic number, mass number, and isotope, and calculate particle counts
- **Objective 2.1d** — Identify periods, groups, and named families on the periodic table

**Concept flow:**
- Dalton's atomic theory → atoms as the fundamental unit of matter
- Subatomic particles → nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons
  - Historical experiments (Thomson: electron mass/charge ratio; Millikan: electron charge;
    Rutherford: nuclear model; Chadwick: neutron) → how the particle model was established
- Atomic number (protons) and mass number (protons + neutrons) → define an element and its
  isotopes
- Periodic table → elements arranged by atomic number into periods and groups sharing similar
  properties

### 2. Molecules and Ions (§2.2)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 2.2a** — Distinguish molecules from ions, and cations from anions
- **Objective 2.2b** — Distinguish molecular from empirical formulas, and determine ionic-compound formulas by charge balance

**Concept flow:**
- Atoms combine → molecules (neutral, covalently bonded) or ions (charged, from electron
  loss/gain)
  - Molecules → diatomic vs. polyatomic; organic compounds and functional groups
  - Ions → cations (electron loss) vs. anions (electron gain); monatomic vs. polyatomic
- Chemical formulas → molecular formula (exact atom count) vs. empirical formula (simplest
  whole-number ratio)
  - Ionic compound formulas → determined by charge balance between cation(s) and anion(s)

### 3. Naming Compounds (§2.3)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 2.3a** — Name ionic compounds, including transition-metal compounds
- **Objective 2.3b** — Name binary molecular compounds using Greek prefixes
- **Objective 2.3c** — Name acids (including oxoacids/oxoanions), bases, and hydrates

**Concept flow:**
- Ionic compounds → metal cation name + nonmetal "-ide" anion name (Roman numeral for variable-
  charge metals)
- Molecular compounds → Greek prefixes indicate atom count when more than one compound can form
  from the same elements
- Acids → binary acids ("hydro-...-ic") vs. oxoacids (named relative to a reference "-ic" acid,
  with "per-"/"-ous"/"hypo-...-ous" modifications for more/fewer oxygen atoms)
  - Oxoanions → "-ate" (from "-ic" acid) vs. "-ite" (from "-ous" acid) naming
- Bases → named as metal hydroxides
- Hydrates → named with a numerical prefix indicating waters of hydration

## Common Student Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Conceptual repair |
|---|---|
| Confusing mass number (A) with atomic mass (average, decimal-valued — introduced in Ch. 3) | Emphasize that mass number is always a whole number specific to one isotope, while atomic mass (Ch. 3) is a weighted average across all naturally occurring isotopes. |
| Forgetting which polyatomic ions to memorize, or confusing similar names (sulfate vs. sulfite, nitrate vs. nitrite) | Anchor the "-ate has more oxygen than -ite" pattern with a single reference pair (e.g., sulfate/sulfite) before extending it to the full list. |
| Applying Greek-prefix (molecular) naming to ionic compounds, or vice versa | Have students classify a compound as ionic (metal + nonmetal) or molecular (nonmetal + nonmetal) *before* choosing a naming method. |
| Losing track of which "-ic" acid is the naming reference when working out per-/hypo-/-ous names | Keep the five reference oxoacids (carbonic, chloric, nitric, phosphoric, sulfuric) visibly posted, and always count oxygen atoms relative to that reference before naming a related oxoacid or oxoanion. |
| Not reducing an ionic-compound formula to its lowest whole-number ratio after balancing charges | Always check the final formula for a common factor to divide out (e.g., Pb₂O₄ → PbO₂) before reporting it. |
