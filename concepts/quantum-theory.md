## Chapter Summary

Chapter 7 shifts the course from macroscopic reactions and energetics to the microscopic structure
of the atom itself, specifically its electrons. It begins with light: wave properties (wavelength,
frequency), the realization that light also behaves as discrete photons (E = hν), and the
photoelectric effect as direct evidence for photon behavior. Bohr's model then explains atomic
emission spectra as a consequence of electrons dropping between quantized energy levels, before
being superseded by full quantum mechanics — the electron's wave nature, the Heisenberg uncertainty
principle, and the Schrödinger equation's probabilistic "electron density" picture.

The chapter's payoff is the electron configuration of atoms: the four quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ,
mₛ) label every electron's "address"; the shapes and energy ordering of s, p, d, f orbitals follow
from those numbers; and the Pauli exclusion principle, Hund's rule, and the Aufbau principle
together determine how electrons fill orbitals from hydrogen up through the transition metals. This
electron-configuration result is the single most important input to Chapter 8's periodic trends and
Chapter 9's bonding theory — nearly everything about *why* the periodic table looks the way it does
traces back to the orbital-filling patterns built here.

## Core Dependency Chain

- **Photon energy (E = hν) and the photoelectric effect**
  - This enables: the Bohr model's explanation of emission spectra (energy of an emitted photon =
    energy difference between orbits).
- **Bohr's theory and atomic emission spectra**
  - This enables: the transition to full quantum mechanics (wave-particle duality, the
    Heisenberg uncertainty principle) as a refinement of the same energy-level picture.
- **Quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ) and atomic orbitals**
  - This enables: electron configuration — every element's configuration is built by assigning
    electrons unique sets of quantum numbers.
- **Electron configuration (Pauli exclusion principle, Hund's rule, Aufbau principle)**
  - This enables:
    - Ch. 8 Periodic Table — every periodic trend (radius, ionization energy, electron affinity)
      is explained in terms of the electron configuration built here.
    - Ch. 9 Chemical Bonding — valence electrons (the outermost-shell electrons identified via
      electron configuration) are what Lewis structures and bonding theory describe.

### 1. Waves and Photons (§7.1)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 7.1a** — Relate wave speed, wavelength, and frequency
- **Objective 7.1b** — Calculate photon energy
- **Objective 7.1c** — Apply the photoelectric effect equation

**Concept flow:**
- Wave properties (wavelength, frequency, amplitude, speed) → electromagnetic radiation (light
  speed c = λν)
- Light as a stream of photons → photon energy E = hν = hc/λ
- Photoelectric effect (electrons ejected only above a threshold frequency) → direct evidence that
  light behaves as discrete photons, not only as a continuous wave

### 2. Bohr's Theory and the Dual Nature of the Electron (§7.2)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 7.2a** — Apply Bohr's theory to hydrogen's energy levels and emission spectrum
- **Objective 7.2b** — Calculate an electron's de Broglie wavelength

**Concept flow:**
- Bohr model (quantized orbits, ΔE = hν = R_H(1/n_i² − 1/n_f²)) → explains atomic (line) emission
  spectra
- De Broglie wavelength (λ = h/mu) → the electron has wave character, refining the Bohr picture
- Heisenberg uncertainty principle → position and momentum cannot both be known precisely
- Schrödinger equation and electron density (ψ²) → the modern, probabilistic description of where
  an electron is likely to be found (the atomic orbital)

### 3. Electrons in Atoms (§7.3)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 7.3a** — Identify valid quantum-number sets and orbital shapes/energies
- **Objective 7.3b** — Write ground-state electron configurations
- **Objective 7.3c** — Use noble-gas-core notation and recognize transition-metal exceptions

**Concept flow:**
- Four quantum numbers → n (energy/size), l (shape: s, p, d, f), mₗ (orientation), mₛ (spin)
  - Each unique combination = one electron's "address"
- Atomic orbitals (s, p, d shapes) and their energy ordering (1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < …) in
  many-electron atoms
- Pauli exclusion principle (no two electrons share all four quantum numbers) + Hund's rule
  (maximize parallel spins in degenerate orbitals) → build electron configurations one element at a
  time
- Aufbau principle + noble-gas-core notation → electron configurations of larger atoms, including
  the 3d/4s irregularities in transition metals (e.g., Cr, Cu)

## Common Student Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Conceptual repair |
|---|---|
| Confusing E = hν with E = hc/λ, or mixing up which variable (ν or λ) is given | Always write both forms of the equation and identify which quantity (wavelength or frequency) the problem provides before substituting. |
| Assuming higher n always means higher l or a larger orbital count without checking the n-dependent rule (l = 0 to n−1) | Explicitly derive allowed l, mₗ values from n each time using the stated rules, rather than memorizing specific cases only. |
| Forgetting Hund's rule and pairing electrons in degenerate orbitals before every orbital has one electron | Have students fill orbital diagrams one electron at a time across all degenerate orbitals first, only pairing once each is singly occupied. |
| Writing an incorrect electron configuration for Cr or Cu (expecting 4s²3d⁴ instead of the actual 4s¹3d⁵) | Flag Cr and Cu explicitly as memorized exceptions caused by the extra stability of half-filled/completely filled d subshells. |
| Confusing the *quantum number* n with the *number of orbitals* or *number of electrons* in a shell | Keep the three related-but-distinct formulas visibly separate: subshells per shell = n; orbitals per subshell = 2l+1; maximum electrons per shell = 2n². |
