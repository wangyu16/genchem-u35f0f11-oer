## Chapter Summary

Chapter 9 asks why atoms bond at all, and answers it with the drive toward a noble-gas electron
configuration. It introduces Lewis dot symbols as a bookkeeping device for valence electrons, then
develops the two limiting bonding types: the ionic bond (complete electron transfer, held together
by electrostatic attraction, quantified by lattice energy) and the covalent bond (electron
sharing). Electronegativity — built directly from Chapter 8's ionization energy and electron
affinity trends — supplies the criterion for deciding which type of bond forms, and how polar a
covalent bond is.

The chapter's technical core is Lewis structure construction: a step-by-step method for drawing
structures, extended by formal charge (choosing the best among several valid structures), resonance
(when no single structure suffices), and the recognized exceptions to the octet rule (incomplete
octets, odd-electron radicals, and expanded octets for period-3-and-beyond elements). Bond enthalpy
closes the chapter as a way to estimate reaction enthalpies from bond-breaking/bond-forming energy.
A correct Lewis structure from this chapter is the mandatory starting point for Chapter 10's
molecular geometry and hybridization.

### 1. Ionic Bond and Covalent Bond (§9.1)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 9.1a** — Draw Lewis dot symbols and explain ionic bonding and lattice energy
- **Objective 9.1b** — Classify bond type using electronegativity difference

**Concept flow:**
- Lewis dot symbol (element symbol + valence electron dots) → basis for describing all bonding
- Ionic bond (electron transfer, electrostatic attraction) → lattice energy (larger for
  higher-charge, smaller ions)
- Covalent bond (electron sharing) → single/double/triple bonds; multiple bonds are shorter and
  generally stronger than single bonds
- Electronegativity (ability to attract shared electrons, built from ionization energy + electron
  affinity trends) → electronegativity difference classifies bond type (ionic ≥ 2.0; polar
  covalent 0.3–2.0; covalent < 0.3)

### 2. Lewis Structures (§9.2)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 9.2a** — Draw Lewis structures using the standard procedure
- **Objective 9.2b** — Calculate formal charge
- **Objective 9.2c** — Identify and explain resonance structures

**Concept flow:**
- Step-by-step Lewis structure method (skeletal structure → count valence electrons → single
  bonds + complete octets on outer atoms → add multiple bonds if central atom is electron-
  deficient)
- Formal charge (valence electrons − lone-pair electrons − ½ bonding electrons) → distinguishes
  between multiple valid Lewis structures
- Resonance (two or more valid Lewis structures for one molecule, none fully accurate alone) →
  the true structure is a blend, not an oscillation
- Octet-rule exceptions → incomplete octet (e.g., BF₃), odd-electron radicals (e.g., NO, NO₂),
  expanded octet (period 3+ central atoms using d orbitals, e.g., SF₆)

### 3. Exceptions to the Octet Rule and Bond Enthalpy (§9.3)

**Learning objectives:**
- **Objective 9.3a** — Identify octet-rule exceptions
- **Objective 9.3b** — Calculate reaction enthalpy from bond enthalpies

**Concept flow:**
- Bond enthalpy (energy to break one mole of a specific bond) → varies slightly by molecular
  environment, so average values are tabulated for polyatomic molecules
- ΔH ≈ Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed) → an estimate of reaction enthalpy using bond-level data,
  complementing Chapter 6's formation-enthalpy method

## Common Student Bottlenecks

| Bottleneck | Conceptual repair |
|---|---|
| Treating the ionic/covalent/polar-covalent classification as three sharp categories rather than a continuum | Emphasize that electronegativity difference is a continuous scale, and the 0.3/2.0 cutoffs are practical guidelines, not hard boundaries. |
| Forgetting to include lone pairs on outer atoms while focused on completing the central atom's octet | Require students to complete every outer atom's octet with lone pairs *before* checking whether the central atom needs a multiple bond. |
| Calculating formal charge incorrectly by not dividing bonding electrons in half | Have students write out the formal-charge formula symbolically (valence − lone-pair electrons − ½ bonding electrons) every time, rather than counting informally. |
| Believing a molecule with resonance structures "switches" between them over time | State explicitly that resonance structures are a human notational limitation — the real molecule has one fixed, blended structure, not an alternating one. |
| Applying the octet rule rigidly to period-3-and-beyond central atoms that legitimately expand their octet | Teach expanded octets as an explicit, row-based rule: only elements in period 3 or below can exceed eight electrons, using available d orbitals. |
